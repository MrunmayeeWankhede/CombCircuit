# Research plan

## Question

When do spatial gradients in the reconstructed ANN arbors produce perturbation
responses that an equipotential-cell model cannot reproduce under matched
assumptions? A biological interpretation requires further measurements.

## Candidate contribution

Syncytial anatomy, the circuit and a proposed coordinating role are already
published. Compartmental cable modeling is established. The potential contribution
is a quantified test of model reduction for this particular circuit and an
experimentally distinguishable perturbation signature. An exhaustive prior-art
search has not established novelty; do not claim to be first.

## First milestone: this prototype

Pinned contacts, source arbors, version caveats, matched models, synthetic pulse,
feedback removal, cable cut, sensitivity sweep, numerical checks and an offline
explorer. This is infrastructure for a study, not the biological result.

## Anatomy gate

1. Confirm the release-matched morphology and coordinates. Obtain explicit
   anastomotic-loop information or a validated mesh where available.
2. Verify radii and soma surface areas rather than assigning full biological
   credibility to the uniform-radius tree.
3. Obtain quadrant annotations and reproduce every Figure 3 matrix entry.
4. Resolve the missing endpoint records and uncertain continuations.
5. Audit the functional recordings for independent animals, repeat recordings,
   sampling intervals and preprocessing. Never treat repeat recordings as
   independent biological samples.

If full geometry remains unavailable, narrow the claim to exported-arbor models.
Do not fill gaps with geometrically convenient connections.

## Mechanism comparison

Test multiple input sites and amplitudes, class-specific contact signs and
strengths, time constants and delays. Compare intrinsic rhythm generation,
bridge-mediated feedback and coordination in the ciliary system; the anatomy
alone does not choose among these mechanisms.

Calibrate on the same observations and reserve separate conditions for evaluation.
Use comparable fitting budgets and account for extra flexibility. If comparing
to ciliary behavior, justify an observation model from neural activity to ciliary
dynamics. Voltage is not beat frequency, and this mapping may be non-identifiable.

## Experiment selection

Look for interventions where plausible models agree at baseline but disagree on
response onset, recovery, spatial asymmetry or (with an observation model)
ciliary arrest/re-beat timing. Rank separation relative to parameter uncertainty
and experimental feasibility. Current local pulse and cut choices are algorithmic
probes, not experimentally optimized interventions.

A useful outcome could be a specific pair of stimulation sites whose responses
distinguish competing mechanisms across plausible parameters. If none exists,
report that and identify the measurement needed to resolve the ambiguity.

## Claims and validation

A fitted animation is not a discovery. Success is a reproducible finding about
when simpler models suffice, or a robust falsifiable perturbation prediction.
Do not claim whole-animal emulation, verified learning, consciousness or proof of
an evolutionary origin. Contacting researchers should follow review and explicit
authorization of the actual message.
